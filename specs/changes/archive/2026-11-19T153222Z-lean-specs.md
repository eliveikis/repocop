## Requirement

The repo template's specs rule sets what a spec says, at the level of a user story, and what the work's own documentation says, so each fact sits at one level, in one place. It keeps acceptance criteria to outcomes, raises the bar for decision records to architecturally significant choices, and makes the specs the truth over any record. Every repository gets the rule on its next template sync, and the skills that write specs and review work follow it.

Today design text drifts to the wrong level. Specs fill with mechanism the code owns, such as timeouts, call counts and fields, while code docs retell the product's design. Acceptance criteria list the steps of the work, and fold-in copies them into the specs. Decision records pile up: the rule asks for one for any "significant, hard-to-reverse choice", so records get written for how features behave, restate the specs, and stay beside them after later records replace parts of them. An agent that follows a citation then reads history it takes for design.

How a feature behaves goes in an area spec, and a repository's area specs are its few broad areas, not one per feature, which could be as small as a button. When a change's behaviour fits none of them, the developer decides where it goes.

## Acceptance Criteria

1. `.claude/rules/specs.md`, in RepoCop and in the repo template, says what the Implementation Notes give. Its other bullets are unchanged.
2. `.claude/rules/principles.md`, in both, says that code docs keep to their own part and don't retell the product's design.
3. Nothing in RepoCop or the template still describes specs, area specs or decision records by the old rule, or contradicts the new one: not `/implement`, `/complete-change`, `review-requirement`, `.claude/rules/reviews.md`, `specs/changes/README.md`, the specs indexes, the introductions of the `architecture.md` files, or `CLAUDE.md`.
4. `/implement` puts a design decision in the spec at the rule's level, and its mechanism in the work's own docs. `/complete-change` folds in only what the Requirement and its criteria establish, at that level, and nothing from Implementation Notes. Both put a feature's behaviour in the area spec it belongs to, never in `architecture.md`, and ask the developer where it goes when it fits none and the change doesn't say.
5. `review-requirement` reports a criterion that is a step of the work or something every delivery does anyway, and a change whose behaviour fits no area spec and doesn't say where it goes. A branch review reports a spec that gains mechanism, and code docs that gain the product's design.
6. A new repository's `specs/architecture.md` stub prompts for what the rule says `architecture.md` holds, in terms that fit writing and media as well as software, as it does now.
7. RepoCop's and the template's `.claude/settings.json` deny Claude Code's file tools reading anything in `specs/decisions/archive/`, so every repository that syncs the template gets the entry. In a Claude Code session, reading or searching a file there with Read or Grep is refused.
8. In RepoCop, `make check` fails when:
   - a record in `specs/decisions/` or its archive is misnamed or untitled, or shares its number with another;
   - a cited ADR id has no record in either folder;
   - anything but a record or a change cites an archived record.

## Out of scope

- Moving an onboarded repository's records or restructuring its specs. Its `specs/index.md` and `specs/architecture.md` are starter files, so they keep their wording until its developer changes them; where they differ from the rule, the rule holds.
- Keeping Bash commands such as `cat` or `grep -r` out of the archive, which a rule on the file tools doesn't do, as for `.env` files.
- A check in the template: criterion 8's checks are RepoCop's own.
- RepoCop's own specs, docs and records, which `area-specs` and `archive-decision-records` bring under the rule.

## Implementation Notes

- **The rule.** Its first three bullets become these five:
  - **Root design files:** `specs/product.md` (why, for whom) and `specs/architecture.md` (how) state the current truth and stand alone. Edit them in place, with no history, decisions log or open questions. `architecture.md` says how the work is built: its goals, its main parts and what each may depend on, the contracts between them, its key flows, and where things live. It gives each part its role in a few lines and links to the area spec for how it behaves, rather than saying it. In code, its parts are components; in writing or media, they are such as sections, series and assets.
  - **What a spec says.** A spec says what a part does and for whom, why, the rules it must keep, its key flows, and the contracts that something outside the work relies on, at the level of a user story. How the part does it, such as its limits, numbers, fields and steps, is the work's own: in code, each module, function and config key documents its responsibility, what it guarantees and how (`.claude/rules/principles.md`). What is too small to be design is stated only by the work and its checks. The level a fact belongs at decides where it goes, not where else it is already written.
  - **Area specs** (`specs/<area>.md`) each describe one of a repository's few broad areas, never a single feature, and are linked from `specs/index.md`. An area spec is the one place its area's behaviour is stated, with the reasons that aren't plain from it. When a change's behaviour fits no area, the developer decides where it goes: an agent doesn't start an area spec on its own, or put behaviour in `architecture.md`.
  - **Decision records** (`specs/decisions/adr-<nnn>-<slug>.md`, titled `# ADR-<nnn>: <the decision>`) say why an architecturally significant choice was made: one that many later changes must work within and that is costly to undo. In code, examples are a boundary between components or what one may depend on, a contract between them (a protocol, a stored format, a determinism rule), the platform, the engine or a core library, and how environments are built. In writing or media, they are such as the formats the work is published in, the toolchain that builds it, and the structure every part follows. How a feature behaves is not one, however hard it was to settle: its area spec says it, and a smaller choice's reason goes in the commit message. Write a record when its choice is made, numbered after the highest in `specs/decisions/` and its archive, as `git ls-files specs/decisions` lists them: status and date, context, decision, consequences, and alternatives rejected with why.
  - **A record is history; the specs are the truth.** Where a record and a spec differ, the spec holds, and no change reconciles older records with it. Once accepted, a record is edited only to say what superseded it: a line at its top names the record that replaced a part of it. A record replaced whole, or whose choice isn't architecturally significant, moves to `specs/decisions/archive/` when a change supersedes it or the developer asks. Records name each other by id (`ADR-029`), never by a link, so a move breaks nothing. Read a record only before changing what it decided. Never load the archive, or cite an archived record anywhere but in another record.
- **The change format's criteria** become: "a numbered list of outcomes the beneficiary can observe, one per item, so each can be cited by its number. The beneficiary may be a tool or code calling an API. Not the steps of the work, which tests to write (`.claude/rules/test-policy.md` decides that), or what every delivery does anyway, such as passing the checks: technical direction the work must follow goes in Implementation Notes." The Changes bullet's last clause becomes "what lasts is in the specs". These are the rule's only other edits.
- **`principles.md`**'s "Name things by their responsibility" bullet adds that a docstring or comment says how its own part works, not the product's design, and links to the spec for why.
- **The skills and `reviews.md`** point at the specs rule rather than restating it. `review-requirement`'s Format item covers criteria that are steps or ritual, and its Gaps item where the behaviour goes. `/implement`'s Record step writes at both levels. `/complete-change`'s test for what to fold in becomes the rule's level, in place of "would someone changing this system a year from now need this", and its step 4 asks the developer when no area fits. `reviews.md` gains the level check for a branch's specs and docs.
- **The archive deny rule.** Add `Read(./specs/decisions/archive/**)` to `permissions.deny`, beside the `.env` entries. Archived changes stay readable, because skills read the changes a branch archived. Check on the installed Claude Code which form of the pattern matches however the session was started, as the session hook was checked on 2.1.312, and use that form.
- **The checks** are cases in `tests/test_specs.py`, which already checks the records in `specs/decisions/`: they extend to its archive. A citation is a bare `ADR-<nnn>` or a link to an archived record's file. The third check skips `specs/decisions/` and `specs/changes/`: records keep the citations they were accepted with, an archived change is never edited, and a change in `todo/` is its developer's to fix.
